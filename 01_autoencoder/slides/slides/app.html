A screening tool for weld inspectors

WHO

A weld inspector checking structural steel

UPLOADS

X-ray tiles of welds

GETS BACK

A risk level and a heatmap of where to look

LOW

91% of sound tiles: skip

CHECK

Borderline: a human looks

HIGH

85–92% of each defect: inspect first

Only 0.5% of defects end up in “low”. A screening aid, not a verdict: the inspector decides.

Who: a weld inspector checking structural steel. What they upload: X-ray tiles of welds. What the model answers: a score, turned into three risk levels, plus the error map as a heatmap of where to look. The two thresholds were chosen on validation data. The low/check boundary is set so that 98% of defects score above it: safety first, because a missed crack costs more than a false alarm. The check/high boundary is set so that only 0.5% of sound tiles reach high. On the unseen test welds, 91% of sound tiles are low and can be skipped, 85 to 92% of each defect type is high, and only 0.5% of defect tiles end up in low. One sentence: the score says how unlike a sound weld the tile looks, the risk level says whether to skip it, check it or check it first, and the heatmap shows where. It does not replace the inspector: acceptance under welding standards depends on defect size and plate thickness, which the model does not measure.
